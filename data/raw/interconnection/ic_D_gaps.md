## Corrections to the brief's assumptions
- **PA's "200%" sizing rule is real but its legal basis is narrower than implied.** It comes from the AEPS Act's statutory definition of "customer-generator" (73 P.S. § 1648.2, raised from 110% to 200%), NOT from an enforceable cap directly in 52 Pa. Code § 75.13. The PUC tried to write a 200% cap directly into the regulation in 2016; the Independent Regulatory Review Commission (IRRC) found the PUC lacked authority to do so and struck it. Net effect for the tool: the 200% figure should still be usable, but cite the statute, not the regulation, and flag that regulatory enforcement mechanics are less settled than a straightforward "regulation says X" citation would suggest.
- **MD's PSC storage pilot program is not literally a "BYOD" (Bring Your Own Device) tariff at BGE/Pepco/Delmarva/SMECO** as characterized in the brief. What exists: (1) the 2019–2024 SB 573 Energy Storage Pilot Program, which built utility-owned/co-developed grid-asset batteries at BGE (Fairhaven, Chesapeake Beach), Pepco (National Harbor), Potomac Edison (Little Orleans, Myersville) — NOT customer-owned; (2) Delmarva's pilot specifically WAS a customer-sited residential VPP (Sunverge, 110 homes in Elk Neck, aggregated 0.5MW/1.5MWh) — the closest real match to "BYOD"; (3) SMECO was not among the four original 2020 pilot filers (BGE, Pepco, Delmarva, Potomac Edison only) — could not confirm SMECO storage tariff participation; (4) the newer DRIVE Act EDSSS pilots (BGE, Pepco, Potomac Edison, ~185.7 MW combined target, filed per a 7/1/2025 Commission directive) ARE customer-sited-storage/bidirectional-EV facing and are the closest match to an ongoing "BYOD-style" program today, but aren't named BYOD in any source reviewed. Recommend re-verifying the brief's premise against a PSC order before treating "BYOD" as an exact program name in downstream UI copy.

## Live/high-risk dockets flagged (deal-risk items)
- **MD's 3,000 MW aggregate net-metering cap**: 51.23% nominally used (June 2025), but the pending Community Solar interconnection pipeline (~2,911 MW) will nearly exhaust the cap on its own. PSC's November 2025 report to the General Assembly recommends legislative action in the 2026 session. This is the single most time-sensitive number in the whole cluster — re-check before any MD deal screening more than a few months old.
- **NJ's 5.8% net metering cap**: statutory trigger (N.J.S.A. 48:3-87), not a hard cutoff — BPU can keep offering NEM past it at its discretion. BPU held stakeholder workshops in late 2024 on broader NEM reform; current subscription level (% of cap used) was not found in sources reviewed this pass — could not verify.
- **DE's 8% aggregate cap**: raised from 5% to 8% by SB 298 (2022) specifically to buy "2–3 years" before a "net metering 2.0" policy redesign — implies DE was approaching its prior cap and may approach the new one on a similar timeline. Current subscription level not found this pass.

## Confirmed but noteworthy
- **DE (SB 298, 2022) ended annual cash-out**: unused net-metering credits now revert to the utility with NO payout at the end of the annual billing period (previously credits were cashed out at avoided cost). This is a meaningful economic change from the "avoided cost cash-out" pattern common elsewhere in the cluster (PA, NJ, MD, DC all still pay out at avoided-cost/PTC/generation rate). Applies at DEC/DEMEC with certainty (per DEMEC's own 2022 board briefing); Delmarva DE's specific tariff implementation of the same statutory change was not independently confirmed against Delmarva's own tariff text.
- **PA storage retrofit precedent**: PUC's 2022–2024 interconnection report documents Met-Ed approving 2 instances of adding a 10kW battery to an existing net-metered solar interconnection (re-reviewed at a higher level but not disqualified) — concrete evidence PA permits storage-only retrofits onto legacy net-metered PV.
