# Gaps and unverifiable items — Cluster E (VA, WV)

## Virginia

- **Battery-addition-to-existing-NEM-system grandfathering (Dominion, APCo, co-ops):** Could not find a primary source that explicitly states whether adding a battery to an already-interconnected, grandfathered net-metered system (a) requires a brand-new interconnection application, and (b) whether that new application would strip legacy status or trigger the standby charge/NEM 2.0 tariff if the aggregate AC nameplate capacity changes. Industry commentary (Virtue Solar) confirms "any change in system capacity triggers a new interconnection application," which strongly implies re-application risk, but no source directly confirms or denies grandfathering forfeiture specifically for a battery-only addition (no new PV). Recommend follow-up with Dominion's DER Interconnection Parameters Manual and APCo's Net Metering Terms and Conditions directly, or a call to each utility's interconnection desk.
- **`storage_counts_toward_cap`** (whether battery kW counts against the NEM system size cap) is not explicitly addressed in any Virginia source reviewed, for either IOUs or co-ops. Left as `Unclear` at the state level.
- **Dominion / APCo current NEM aggregate-cap subscription level** (% of the 6% cap actually used) was not found published in a citable secondary source as of the research date; the 2026 SCC order commentary states participation is "well under" the cap but gives no percentage. The co-op-level caps (CVEC, BARC, REC) DO have specific published subscription percentages, which are captured.
- **`ic_timeline_days`** (statutory review timeline) for Virginia's 20VAC5-314 Level 1/2/3 process was not confirmed as a specific day-count in the sources reviewed; only fee schedules and equipment certification requirements were confirmed.
- **`grid_charging_allowed`** for Virginia was marked `Yes-but-not-credited` based on general net-metering-plus-TOU practice (e.g., Dominion's Off-Peak Plan crediting exports at the interval rate), but no source explicitly states whether grid-charged battery discharge that is later exported receives net metering credit versus being explicitly excluded. This is an inference, not a directly confirmed value — treat with caution for arbitrage modeling.
- **`non_export_option` specifics** — no Virginia source reviewed (state or utility level) published a defined non-export/limited-export interconnection tariff path with its own kW thresholds or process. This likely exists in utility-specific Distributed Energy Resources interconnection manuals (e.g., Dominion's DER Interconnection Parameters Manual, referenced but not fully read) but was not confirmed in the time available.
- **NOVEC, SVEC, Danville, Manassas** have materially thinner public documentation than Dominion/APCo/CVEC/BARC/REC. Confidence on their utility rows is Low-Medium; credit rates and true-up mechanics in particular are not published on their sites and would need a call to the co-op/utility or a copy of their actual net metering rider/tariff to verify.
- **Virginia Shared Solar Program current subscription/build-out status** for the 2026 expansion (525 MW under new legislation signed ~July 2026) was found at a program level but not broken out precisely by utility for a specific "as of" date within the time available.

## West Virginia

- **`grid_charging_allowed`** left as `Unclear` for all four WV utilities — no source reviewed (state statute, PSC rule 150 CSR 33, or AEP's 2026 Net Metering Customer Information Guide) explicitly addresses whether a grid-charged battery may still export and receive net-metering/export credit.
- **`non_export_option`** — no explicit non-export/limited-export interconnection tariff path was found in West Virginia sources reviewed (state or utility level). AEP's TIIR document (Technical Interconnection and Interoperability Requirements) likely contains this detail for larger DER but was not fully read in the time available.
- **Battery-addition-to-existing-system treatment for BOTH WV grandfathering regimes** (AEP/Wheeling's application-date cutoff of 2/28/2026, and Mon Power/Potomac Edison's 25-year settlement-date cutoff of 3/27/2024) is not explicitly addressed in any source reviewed. This is flagged as the single most valuable unanswered question for the storage retrofit use case in WV, per the task brief — recommend a direct follow-up with AEP-WV's dgcoordinator-wv@aep.com or FirstEnergy's WV interconnection desk.
- **Standby charge in WV:** no evidence was found that any WV utility assesses a Virginia-style demand-based standby charge on net-metered customers. This is treated as "not applicable" rather than "unknown" because the 2025 AEP-WV reform and 2024 Mon Power/Potomac Edison settlement both explicitly restructured the ongoing per-kWh export credit rate as their cost-recovery mechanism, with no standby/demand charge mentioned in either proceeding's coverage.
- **Current WV aggregate cap subscription status** (% of the 3%/0.5% statewide cap used) was not found published for any of the four WV utilities in the sources reviewed.
- **`ic_timeline_days`** for WV: AEP's guide describes multiple 10-business-day review windows at specific process steps (test result review, submittal review, meter installation) but does not publish a single end-to-end statutory timeline; left blank/not published rather than summing the steps into an unverified total.
- **150 CSR 33 full text** was not read directly (only summarized via secondary sources and AEP's customer guide, which cites and paraphrases it); the state-level Level 1/Level 2 fee figures and the 25 kW residential cap were corroborated by AEP's guide (primary-adjacent, utility-published document citing the rule), but a direct read of the Legislative Rule itself would raise confidence from Medium to High for several state-row cells.

## General
